# M9: the full catalog, third-party standards, and a before/after example

## The catalog
17 packs, 119 items. Added since [M8b](m8b.md):

- **The `browser` kind:** a real Chromium (playwright-core with the Playwright cache's Chromium, else Google Chrome, else `STANDARDS_CHROMIUM`). A visit records requests, cookies set before any interaction, console errors, axe-core results and Tab stops. Items judge them with `expect` (`axe`, `console`, `transfer`, `trackers`) or with their own script (`probe(page, visit)` plus a judge). One browser per process: at most 4 contexts at once, 2 pages per site, and it closes when idle.
- **Packs:**
  - `accessibility`: axe, keyboard reach and visible focus, skip link, reduced motion, accessibility statement.
  - `security`: exposed files, CSP, framing, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy, cookie flags, version banners.
  - `content`: expired offers, broken images, placeholders, a contact in every footer, this year in the footer.
  - `i18n`: languages reachable, unknown locales 404, copy in the page's language, a switcher that keeps the page.
  - `local-business`: LocalBusiness/AutoRental JSON-LD per location, location pages, NAP, map link, current hours.
  - `rental`: booking entry, fleet data, FAQPage, rental terms, prices with VAT.
  - `scanners`: Mozilla Observatory, the W3C Nu checker, OSV for locked dependencies, gitleaks for history, the repo's own lint and typecheck.
  - `nextjs` v2 (below).
- **Additions to existing packs:**
  - `eu-consumer`: cookie consent before trackers, Consent Mode v2.
  - `performance`: page weight, console errors, render-blocking third parties, image formats.
  - `website` v4: manifest, viewport, charset, theme-color, domain expiry.
  - `website/security-headers` v2: HSTS for at least a year.

## Third-party standards
- **`sources: [{title, url}]` on an item:** names the official docs, spec or law it follows. 105 of the 119 items cite 184 sources: Google Search Central, MDN, OWASP, WCAG 2.2, W3C WAI, RFCs, ogp.me, schema.org, web.dev, nextjs.org, EUR-Lex, slov-lex. A fix prompt says "Follows: …; where the source and the recipe differ, the source wins". Items with no primary source are listed in the commit that added them.
- **`applies_when: {dependency, version}`:** keeps an item to assets whose checkout uses that dependency in that semver range. It reads the installed version, else the one package.json names.
  - `nextjs` v2 gates every item on `next`, so non-Next repos get no cells. Version-specific ones are also gated by range: async request APIs and removed or deprecated APIs `>=15`, `proxy.ts` `>=16`.
  - Its 15 items come from the Production Checklist, the 15 and 16 upgrade guides, and the API reference pages they link.
- **`standards sources`:** reads every cited page once (anchors share a read), hashes its text without markup and digit runs, and compares the hash with `packs/sources.lock.json`. It reports `new`, `changed`, `blocked` (a firewall answered; the lock keeps its entry), `gone` or `same`. `--update` writes the lock.
  - Run it weekly. A `changed` source means its items may need to follow upstream; `gone` means find where it moved.

## Running safely against live sites
- **Intrusive items** (`intrusive: true`, today `security/exposed-files`) probe paths a firewall reads as an attack, such as `/.env` and `/.git`. They run only when named (`check --item`) or for a business with `intrusive: true`. Vercel's firewall answers such probes by challenging every request from the checker's IP for up to 40 minutes.
- **`get()` keeps at most 4 requests in flight per origin** (`STANDARDS_PER_ORIGIN`). A check that meets a challenge page reports an error, never a pass.

## The example
`examples/` holds a fictional car and van rental, Príklad Rent, in sk and en:
- **Before:** `rental-before/` is a bare `create-next-app`.
- **After:** `rental-after/` is the same scaffold grown to meet every standard that localhost can show.

`bun examples/run.ts` builds both, serves them on :3101 and :3102, checks both businesses with every pack except `repo` (it needs a GitHub repo), writes `examples/results/` and stops the servers, in about 40 s. On 2026-11-21: **before 53 of 104 checks, after 104 of 104.** Seven production-only items are waived for both: TLS, canonical host, SPF/DMARC, domain expiry, CrUX, Lighthouse and Observatory.
